# Great Readings

This document is a curated collection of foundational primary texts, field-defining monographs, and landmark academic essays in Political Science. 

Rather than standard introductory textbooks, these works represent milestone intellectual breakthroughs that established paradigms, introduced enduring conceptual vocabularies, or fundamentally challenged prevailing assumptions about power, governance, and human behavior.

## Foundational Political Philosophy & Normative Thought

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [*The Republic*](https://archive.org/details/republicofplato00platuoft) | Plato | Classic Text | Establishes the foundational Western inquiry into justice, the ideal state, and the structural vulnerabilities of democratic governance. |
| [*The Prince*](https://archive.org/details/prince00machuoft) | Niccolò Machiavelli | Classic Treatise | Initiates modern political realism by divorcing statecraft and the acquisition of political power from Christian moral theology. |
| [*Leviathan*](https://archive.org/details/leviathan00hobbuoft) | Thomas Hobbes | Classic Monograph | Formulates the social contract theory, arguing that an absolute sovereign is necessary to escape the violent chaos of the state of nature. |
| [*Second Treatise of Government*](https://archive.org/details/secondtreatiseof0000lock) | John Locke | Classic Treatise | Lays the philosophical bedrock for classical liberalism, popular sovereignty, natural rights to property, and the right to revolution. |
| [*On Liberty*](https://archive.org/details/onliberty00milluoft) | John Stuart Mill | Philosophical Essay | Formulates the classic defense of free expression, individual autonomy, and the harm principle against the tyranny of the majority. |
| *A Theory of Justice* | John Rawls | Monograph | Revitalized contemporary political philosophy through the "veil of ignorance" and the derivation of egalitarian principles of distributive justice. |
| [*The Wretched of the Earth*](https://archive.org/details/wretchedearth00fano) | Frantz Fanon | Monograph | Provides a seminal psychological and socio-political critique of colonial domination, decolonization, and national liberation movements. |

## State Formation, Regime Dynamics & Comparative Politics

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [*Democracy in America*](https://archive.org/details/democracyinamer01tocqgoog) | Alexis de Tocqueville | Classic Monograph | Offers an enduring sociological and institutional examination of American civic associations, egalitarian culture, and majoritarian risks. |
| *Politics as a Vocation* | Max Weber | Seminal Essay | Introduces the canonical definition of the state as possessing a monopoly on the legitimate use of physical force, contrasting the ethic of ultimate ends with the ethic of responsibility. |
| *War Making and State Making as Organized Crime* | Charles Tilly | Landmark Essay | Challenges benign theories of state origin by demonstrating how European state-building emerged from coercive extraction and war preparation. |
| [*Seeing Like a State: How Certain Schemes to Improve the Human Condition Have Failed*](https://archive.org/details/seeinglikeastate00scot) | James C. Scott | Monograph | Demonstrates how state-driven simplification, legibility, and high-modernist ideology lead to catastrophic social and ecological failures. |
| *States and Social Revolutions: A Comparative Analysis of France, Russia, and China* | Theda Skocpol | Monograph | Develops a structural, non-voluntarist theory of revolutionary change rooted in agrarian class structures and administrative-military state collapse. |

## International Order, Conflict & Global Governance

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [*History of the Peloponnesian War*](https://archive.org/details/historyofpelopon00thuc) | Thucydides | Classic History | Provides the earliest realist exploration of balance-of-power dynamics, alliance traps, and the ruthless logic of power exemplified in the Melian Dialogue. |
| [*Man, the State, and War: A Theoretical Analysis*](https://archive.org/details/manthestateandwar) | Kenneth N. Waltz | Monograph | Introduces the three levels of analysis (the individual, the state, and the international system) to explain the recurring causes of interstate war. |
| *After Hegemony: Cooperation and Discord in the World Political Economy* | Robert O. Keohane | Monograph | Formulates neoliberal institutionalism, demonstrating that international institutions can foster sustainable cooperation even amidst systemic anarchy. |
| [*The Tragedy of Great Power Politics*](https://archive.org/details/tragedyofgreatpo0000mear) | John J. Mearsheimer | Monograph | Articulates offensive realism, arguing that systemic uncertainty relentlessly compels rational great powers to maximize relative power and pursue regional hegemony. |
| *Rationalist Explanations for War* | James D. Fearon | Landmark Paper | Revolutionized security studies by modeling war as a bargaining failure caused by issue indivisibility, asymmetric information with incentives to misrepresent, and commitment problems. |
| *Anarchy is What States Make of It: The Social Construction of Power Politics* | Alexander Wendt | Landmark Paper | Introduced social constructivism to international relations, arguing that international anarchy does not inherently create self-help systems absent shared intersubjective meanings. |

## Institutions, Political Behavior & Public Choice

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| [*The Federalist Papers*](https://archive.org/details/federalistcommen00madi) (Nos. 10 & 51) | James Madison, Alexander Hamilton, & John Jay | Classic Essays | The definitive constitutional theory analyzing how an extended republic and institutional separation of powers control factional violence and ambition. |
| *The Nature of Belief Systems in Mass Publics* | Philip E. Converse | Landmark Paper | Groundbreaking empirical study demonstrating that the majority of citizens lack ideological constraint, stable political opinions, and consistent policy belief systems. |
| *An Economic Theory of Democracy* | Anthony Downs | Monograph | Applies rational choice theory and the spatial median voter theorem to explain electoral competition and rational voter ignorance. |
| *The Logic of Collective Action: Public Goods and the Theory of Groups* | Mancur Olson | Monograph | Demonstrates why rational individuals fail to organize for collective public goods without selective incentives, explaining why concentrated interest groups dominate diffuse majorities. |

## Methodological Foundations & Political Inquiry

| Title | Author(s) | Type | Why It's Worth Reading |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| *Designing Social Inquiry: Scientific Inference in Qualitative Research* | Gary King, Robert O. Keohane, & Sidney Verba | Monograph | The landmark treatise bridging quantitative and qualitative social science by establishing unified standards for descriptive and causal inference. |
| *Thick Description: Toward an Interpretive Theory of Culture* | Clifford Geertz | Seminal Essay | Establishes interpretive social science, arguing that political and social action must be analyzed as contextual systems of symbolic meaning rather than purely mechanistic laws. |
| *The Science of "Muddling Through"* | Charles E. Lindblom | Landmark Paper | Challenges comprehensive rational-planning models by demonstrating that real-world public policy progresses through successive, disjointed incremental adjustments. |
